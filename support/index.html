What the app does

Tennis Quotient rates professional tennis players by the results of their matches. Each match earns points based on whether the player won or lost and on the opponent's rank on the ranking list in force when the tournament started. A player's value is the average points per match. You compare two players from the same tour with Cohen's d, an effect size that measures the difference between their averages in standard deviations.

Common questions

Why isn't the latest tournament in the app?

The data is built into the app and never comes from the network. It is refreshed about weekly through App Store app updates, and the app always shows the date the data runs through: at the top of the Matchup tab and on the Data & credits tab. To get newer data, update the app from the App Store.

Why is a match or an event missing?

Match results and rankings come from BALLDONTLIE (balldontlie.io). The source doesn't cover everything. Association of Tennis Professionals (ATP) results start in 2007; some ATP events are missing from the source, and ATP Challenger events are not included. Women's Tennis Association (WTA) results start in 2024. The Data & credits tab lists the known gaps.

What does "possible retirement" or "possible walkover" mean?

The data source doesn't mark retirements (when a player stops during a match) or walkovers (when a player withdraws before it starts). The app flags a match as a possible retirement when the score isn't a completed match, and as a possible walkover when there's no score. By default, possible retirements count and possible walkovers don't. You can change both on the Tuning tab.

What do the History window, Surface and rank-adjusted values do?

The History window switch on the matchup and result screens picks the matches that count: the last 52 weeks, the full record, or a custom number of weeks, months or matches. Surface lets you give matches on the matchup's surface extra weight, or use only matches on that surface; each surface shows how many matches each player has there, with a warning below 10. Rank-adjusted values, used by default and switchable off on the Tuning tab, pull a player's average toward what players of the same rank usually score when they have only a few matches. The original points table still scores every match. The history, surface and rank-adjustment settings were chosen by testing them on past matches, and the app shows how they were chosen. You can change any of them on the Tuning tab.

How do I read Cohen's d?

A positive d means Player A has the higher average, and a negative d means Player B does. As a rough guide, about 0.2 is a small difference, 0.5 medium and 0.8 large. The confidence interval (CI) shows how precise the estimate is: a wide interval means the result is less certain, usually because there are few matches.

Why use the opponent's rank at the time and not today's rank?

Beating a player who was ranked 5th that week is a different result from beating the same player years later at a lower rank. The app always uses the ranking list dated on or before the start of the tournament.

I changed the points table. How do I go back?

On the Tuning tab, tap Use approved default. Your custom tables stay saved, and you can switch between them at any time. Every saved matchup records which table produced it.

How do I delete my saved matchups?

Your matchups, settings and custom tables are stored only on your iPhone. Deleting the app removes all of them.

Does the app need an internet connection or an account?

No to both. No account, no ads, no tracking. Tennis Quotient works offline, makes no network requests for data and collects no personal information. See the privacy policy.
